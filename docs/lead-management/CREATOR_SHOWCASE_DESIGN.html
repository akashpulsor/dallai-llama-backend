Design doc · Creator Showcase & Outreach · v4

Creator profiles that brands can watch, and mailers that lead there

Creators link their YouTube channel. YouTube hosts and streams every video, and we embed it at its real aspect ratio. Our pages add the brand funnel on top: ranking, likes, requests and mail. Visibility rewards funded films made on the platform, and it adjusts on its own as the platform grows.

Date: 2026-10-08 Revision: v4: every risk closed with a design, no dependency on Google OAuth review Owner service: tenant-service Touches: pre-production, creative-planning, platform-ui, creator-ui, infra-platform, Google Cloud
What changed in v4: the risks are closed.
  • No creator OAuth on the critical path. Platform films can go to an official Dalaillama YouTube channel, uploaded by us through an OAuth app on our own Google Workspace. An internal app needs no Google review, shows no "unverified" screen and has no 7-day token expiry. Creators can also publish to their own channel through the kit. Creator OAuth (Phase Y) is now optional and off the plan.
  • YouTube is replaceable. A VideoHost port with two adapters: YouTube (default) and SELF (MinIO, short-lived signed URLs). We own every platform film's master, so platform films can move off YouTube with one config change.
  • Funded is now verified: the client paid through the review page's pay gate, plus production evidence from the app (generated takes, review sessions, client comments).
  • Mail: 100 free mails per creator per week; past that, creators buy mail packs from their wallet, and that revenue pays for more sending capacity. Recipient data is hashed and raw emails are purged after 30 days. Templates and platform mails only carry fully funded films whose client accepted marketing use at payment. Brands can report a video.
Sections marked V4 changed in this revision; V3 marks the YouTube redesign.
v3 recap (changes from v2).
  • Video storage: R2 and every copy of video bytes are gone. YouTube hosts and streams all video, and we store only video ids.
  • Onboarding: creators link a channel and choose 2 videos (configurable) to start their showcase.
  • Origin: every video is tagged PLATFORM or EXTERNAL. As funded platform films build up, external work loses visibility gradually.
  • Weak profile and bootstrap: both are now defined by a reinforcement ladder (from Skinner's pigeon experiments) and a visibility floor that rises with the platform's own inventory.
  • Publishing platform films: creators publish to their own YouTube channel with our marker. A manual kit works from day one; one-click upload comes after Google approves our OAuth app.
Sections marked V3 are new or rewritten.

§1Summary

  • Creator profile dalaillama.in/c/<handle> is created at subscription. A creator without a channel is asked to create one; it is free and takes a minute. The creator links their YouTube channel; we import their uploads, and they pick at least 2 to showcase. Name and avatar are filled in from the channel.
  • Platform films go to the creator's own YouTube channel, through a prefilled upload kit now and one-click upload later. Our marker and the stored video id let us recognise them as PLATFORM. Everything else is EXTERNAL.
  • Our pages embed YouTube at the video's true aspect ratio (vertical Shorts stay vertical), with "Watch on YouTube" always available. Video bandwidth and storage cost us nothing.
  • Visibility follows a creator ladder and a self-adjusting floor. At launch everyone is shown, because nobody has funded platform films yet. As funded platform films build up, external-only creators sink slowly but never vanish.
  • Brands watch and like without signing in, and sign up (by email link) to follow a creator or request a video. All brand mail goes through the one-a-day delivery engine.
SubscribeProfile + email→ LinkYouTube channel→ Pick2 showcase videos→ MakeFunded platform film→ PublishTo their YouTube→ RiseVisibility reward→ Mail≤1/brand/day→ ConvertRequest → brief

§2Problem, personas, constraints

Creators make good brand films and nobody can find them. Brands will not browse an unknown platform. Email is our cheapest reach, and it needs a landing place that proves the work within ten seconds. Every creator already has, or can make for free, a YouTube channel, which is the cheapest video CDN available.

Constraints

  • No new microservice and minimal cost. YouTube carries the video. Our cost is API quota, which is free at our scale.
  • YouTube's rules bind us. The developer policies govern the player, attribution, data storage (30-day refresh) and derived metrics. See §8.
  • Google OAuth verification takes time. YouTube scopes are sensitive, so day one must not depend on OAuth (§9).
  • One sender reputation, no ffmpeg on request threads, manual ops: unchanged from v2.

§8YouTube as the video layer V3

8.1 What YouTube gives us for free

  • Hosting, transcoding, adaptive streaming and a global CDN. Brands play the video inside our page, and "Watch on YouTube" opens the original.
  • Reads cost 1 quota unit per call (channels.list, playlistItems.list, videos.list with up to 50 ids). The default allowance is 10,000 units a day. Importing a 500-video channel costs about 20 units.
  • Per current docs, uploads (videos.insert) have their own allowance of 100 calls a day by default, and uploads are no longer forced to private for unverified API projects. Re-check both when Phase Y starts, because Google has changed these rules before.
  • status.containsSyntheticMedia on upload, which is YouTube's own AI-content disclosure.

8.2 Policy rules we design around

PolicyWhat we do
Most API data may be stored for at most 30 days, then deleted or refreshedWe store video ids and channel ids (identifiers) permanently. Titles, thumbnails, durations and status are cached in youtube_video and refreshed by a nightly job when older than refresh-after-days (25). Videos that disappear or turn private are hidden from the showcase.
No new or derived metrics from API dataThe ranking uses only our own data: likes, plays and full plays on our pages, brand requests, funded flags and origin. YouTube's view and like counts are never stored, scored or shown.
The player must not be modified or obscured; YouTube must be shown as the sourceStandard IFrame player with no overlays on the video. Cards show a small YouTube mark. A "Watch on YouTube" link sits beside every player.
Player at least 200×200 pxMinimum card width 220 px; a vertical card is at least 220 wide.
Privacy policy that explains what we access, with a revoke linkA new /privacy section for YouTube data and the Google security-settings link. Google's OAuth verification requires it anyway.

8.3 Aspect ratio

  • Each item stores aspect_w and aspect_h. Platform films take these from our render; external videos from videos.list?part=player&maxHeight=720, which returns embedWidth/embedHeight scaled to the true ratio. If no size comes back, the import marks the item UNKNOWN_RATIO and shows it as 16:9.
  • The player container is CSS aspect-ratio: w / h, capped at 80 vh tall. Vertical videos (ratio below 1) render as vertical cards in the grid, two rows tall, and open in a vertical modal on desktop and full screen on phones.
  • The "Watch on YouTube" link is youtube.com/shorts/{id} for vertical videos up to 3 minutes and youtube.com/watch?v={id} otherwise.
  • Player options: playsinline=1, rel=0, muted autoplay only on hover previews. Events: PLAYING for 3 s counts as a play, ENDED as a full play, both sent to our /plays endpoint.

§9Connecting a channel, and publishing to it V3

9.1 How the creator logs in and proves the channel is theirs

The creator keeps logging in with Keycloak. YouTube is linked to their account afterwards, in one of two ways:

9.2 Import and first picks

  1. Get the channel's uploads playlist id from channels.list part=contentDetails,snippet. The display name and avatar are filled in from the channel's snippet if empty.
  2. Page through playlistItems.list, 50 at a time, up to import-max-videos (500), then call videos.list part=snippet,contentDetails,status,player in batches of 50.
  3. Mark each video eligible or not, with the reason: public, embeddable, not age-restricted, not made for kids, duration within bounds.
  4. The creator sees a grid of eligible videos with their ratio badge and must pick at least min-initial-picks (2). Each pick gets an industry, a format and a client label, plus the rights checkbox. Picks are capped at max-external-picks (6), so creators show their best rather than everything.
  5. Re-sync is manual (a button), plus the nightly refresh for cached fields.

9.3 Publishing a platform film V4

The film bar's "Publish" dialog offers two destinations. Neither needs Google to review our app.

Path 1: Dalaillama's official channel (one click). tenant-service holds one refresh token, for our own channel only, issued by an OAuth client whose consent screen is Internal to our Google Workspace (§21.1). A single worker streams the film from its presigned MinIO URL into YouTube's resumable upload. It sets the title, a description crediting the creator (Made by <name> on Dalaillama · dalaillama.in/c/<handle>), containsSyntheticMedia=true and public visibility. The returned id is stored, so the film is PLATFORM by construction. If Google ever forces API uploads to private, ops uploads in YouTube Studio and pastes the link, which goes through the same match check as Path 2.

Path 2: the creator's own channel (upload kit):

  1. Download the film (the creator's existing presigned link).
  2. Copy a prefilled title, a description whose first line is our marker Made on Dalaillama · dalaillama.in/c/<handle>, and tags dalaillama, ai video.
  3. A checklist step: "In YouTube Studio, set Altered or synthetic content to Yes". This is YouTube's disclosure rule for realistic AI video.
  4. Paste the YouTube link back. We verify it (§9.4) and create a PLATFORM showcase item linked to the project.

Phase Y (optional, only if creators ask), one-click to the creator's own channel: the same metadata is set through videos.insert, with status.containsSyntheticMedia=true, selfDeclaredMadeForKids=false, and privacy as the creator chooses (public or unlisted). A worker streams the file from the presigned URL to YouTube's resumable upload, one at a time. The returned id is stored, so recognition is certain.

An import never turns an EXTERNAL video into PLATFORM, even if it contains the marker. A video only becomes PLATFORM by being linked to a project, which stops people pasting the marker into old videos.

§10Visibility and ranking: the reinforcement model V3

You asked for a system that starts with no platform videos and shifts visibility toward creators who make funded films on our platform, using the pigeon experiment. That is B. F. Skinner's operant-conditioning work: a behaviour that is reinforced, ideally immediately, becomes more frequent. Rewards can be built up step by step (shaping), and a behaviour that is no longer rewarded fades slowly (extinction). We apply it in the open: the rules are shown to creators on a "How visibility works" page. Motivation comes from clear, earnable rewards, not hidden tricks.

10.1 The behaviour we reinforce

The behaviour is to finish a client-funded film on the platform and publish it. Linking a channel, curating picks and completing the profile are the earlier steps that lead up to it.

10.2 Shaping: the creator ladder (this defines "weak")

L0 · HIDDENNo verified channel, or fewer than 2 picks
Profile page shows "Coming soon"; noindex
L1 · STARTERChannel verified, ≥2 picks, name + avatar + headline + industry
On Discover; on the landing page only while the floor is L1
L2 · MAKER≥1 PLATFORM film published
Platform badge; exploration slots
L3 · PROVEN≥1 verified-funded PLATFORM film in the last 90 days (§21.4)
Eligible for landing and automatic picks at any stage
L4 · STAR≥3 funded in 90 days, or ≥2 converted brand requests
Highest standing; first in automatic picks

Weak profile = a creator below the current visibility floor (§10.3). It is relative on purpose. At launch nobody is weak, because the floor is L1. Later, an L1 creator who never makes platform films becomes weak. The thresholds per level are in config.

10.3 Bootstrap: a floor that rises with inventory

The nightly ranking run computes the floor separately for each surface (landing, Discover "Top", automatic picks):

floor(surface) = the highest level L such that creators at level ≥ L supply at least
  fill-factor × surface-size eligible items (fill-factor 1.5; landing size 12 → 18 items)
  • Day one: everyone is L1, so the floor is L1 and the landing page is full of external work picked by creators.
  • As funded platform films arrive, once creators at L3 and above can fill 18 landing items on their own, the landing floor becomes L3 by itself. External-only creators move to Discover. Nobody flips a switch.
  • No cliff: the floor changes by at most one level per run, and the "All" tab on Discover always lists everyone at L1 and above.

10.4 Item score: platform maturity and origin decay

maturity m = min(1, funded PLATFORM films published in last 90 days / maturity-target) (target 40)
originFactor = 1.0 for PLATFORM; 1 − m × (1 − external-floor) for EXTERNAL (external-floor 0.3)
standingFactor = level weight × reinforcement decay
  level weights L1 0.6 · L2 0.8 · L3 1.0 · L4 1.1
  decay = 0.5 ^ (days since last funded PLATFORM film / 60), never below 0.5; = 1 for L1 and L2
quality = 0.30 funded + 0.20 likeRate + 0.15 completionRate + 0.25 brandIntent + 0.10 freshness (as v2, our own data only)
score = quality × originFactor × standingFactor

At launch m = 0, so external videos are not penalised, and with no engagement yet the order comes from creator picks and freshness. As the platform matures, external videos fall to 30% weight. Creators who keep finishing funded films hold their standing; ones who stop decay gently toward half.

10.6 Assembling the landing grid (12)

  1. Pinned ids from config. 2. Active spotlights. 3. Exploration slots. 4. The rest by score from creators at or above the floor, at most 2 per creator and 4 per industry, with vertical and horizontal mixed so the grid stays balanced. The section is hidden while fewer than 6 items pass the gates.

§11One mail a day per brand: the delivery engine

Unchanged from v2. Every non-transactional brand mail (manual, automatic, follow) becomes an intent. The guard refuses suppressed recipients, the same creator mailing the same brand within 14 days, and anything over the caps.

  • Fast path: a manual intent to a brand with no mail in the last 24 hours and nothing else queued is sent immediately from the creator's own address.
  • Daily digest at 13:00 UTC (18:30 IST): one solo mail or one platform digest (up to 4 cards) per recipient. Manual intents first, then follow, then automatic. Leftovers roll over and expire after 3 days.
  • UNIQUE (recipient_email, delivery_day) guarantees one mail per day.

Mail cards use the YouTube thumbnail, which is allowed while cached for 30 days or less, with a "▶ Watch" button that opens our profile page, because attribution and the request button live there. Automatic picks now draw from creators at or above the automatic-picks floor and prefer spotlit films.

§18Rules and edge cases

AreaRule
One channel per creatorchannel_id is unique. A second creator claiming the same channel is refused with "This channel is linked to another account; contact support".
Channel goes awayThe refresh job finds the video private or deleted → gone_at is set → the item becomes UNAVAILABLE and drops from every surface. If the channel loses the code after verification, nothing happens; it was proven once.
Embedding blocked lateronError codes 101/150 in the player → hide the card for that session and report it. The next refresh marks the item.
ThumbnailsUse thumbnail_url from the API cache (YouTube's own image host). Never copy images to our storage.
No YouTube statsView and like counts are not fetched, stored or shown, so the derived-metrics clause never applies.
OAuth tokensRefresh tokens are encrypted with AES-GCM and a key from a k8s secret, and never leave tenant-service. On revoke or invalid_grant → status REVOKED and the creator is asked to reconnect. The state parameter is HMAC-signed with the tenant id and a 10-minute expiry.
UploadsOne worker, one upload at a time, resumable. Retry 3 times, then FAILED with a reason. Quota is checked before starting. containsSyntheticMedia=true is always set.
Floor changesAt most one level per run. Every run is written to showcase_ranking_run, so you can see why the landing page looked the way it did on any date.
Public payloadsOnly publicId, handle, youtubeVideoId and display fields; never internal ids or emails.
RightsThe rights checkbox applies to EXTERNAL picks too (a client's ad on the creator's channel).

§21Risks and how each is closed V4

21.1 Google OAuth trust: removed from the critical path

A new app asking strangers for YouTube access is the hardest case for Google review: sensitive scopes, a scary warning screen, and weeks of back-and-forth. The design now needs no third-party OAuth at all.

NeedHow it works without review
Prove a creator owns a channelA code in the channel description, read with an API key. No OAuth.
Read channel videosPublic data through the API key. No OAuth.
Upload platform films with zero creator effortUpload to our own channel. The Google Cloud project sits in a Google Workspace org (Business Starter, one seat) whose user owns the "Dalaillama" brand channel. The OAuth consent screen is set to Internal, so only accounts in our org can authorise. Google's help centre lists this as exempt from verification ("The app is only used by people in your Google Workspace or Cloud Identity organization. The project must be owned by the organization, and its OAuth Consent Screen must be configured for internal use"), so it has no unverified screen and no 100-user cap. The 7-day token expiry applies only to external apps in Testing. The Cloud project must be created inside the Workspace org, not under a personal Gmail account. Ops authorises once, and the refresh token goes into a k8s secret.
Put a film on the creator's own channelThe upload kit plus a pasted link. The creator uploads in YouTube Studio as usual.
If Google ever forces API uploads to private againThe job moves to NEEDS_MANUAL, ops uploads that film in Studio and pastes the link, and it is matched like a kit link. Nothing is blocked.

Week-1 checklist (about 2 hours of ops work): create the Workspace (in the Admin console, turn on YouTube and Brand Accounts for the org) and the brand channel; create the Cloud project and enable YouTube Data API v3; restrict the API key to that API and the box IP; set the consent screen to Internal with scope youtube.upload; run the consent once; store YT_OFFICIAL_REFRESH_TOKEN and YOUTUBE_API_KEY; upload one probe video. Creator OAuth (Phase Y) waits until the app has a public track record (privacy policy, terms, live users), which makes external verification much easier if creators ever ask for it.

Bonus: the official channel becomes Dalaillama's own marketing channel, full of real client work, each video crediting and linking to its creator.

21.3 Creators without a channel

The wizard asks them to create one, with YouTube's two-minute guide linked. Until they do, they stay L0 for EXTERNAL picks. Their platform films can still go on the official channel, so a creator without a channel is never blocked from showing platform work.

Broken embeds: the player's onError (101/150) hides the card for the session and reports it; the nightly refresh marks the item UNAVAILABLE. Films on the official channel are under our control, so they do not break this way.

21.4 "Funded" is verified, not claimed

Funding is unambiguous in today's flow. A package only locks through the review page's pay gate (/lock/verify captures the client's Razorpay payment, and /lock/settled is refused while any balance is due). Locked therefore means fully paid by the client. On top of that, FundingVerifier checks production evidence that only real work in the app produces. pre-production returns raw counts and the thresholds live in config:

SignalDefault thresholdSource
Client lock (fully paid)requiredprojects.client_locked_at
Generated takes on the project≥ 5shot clip versions
Client review sessions≥ 1reviews start/end
Client comments or direction approvals≥ 1review comments, creative-direction approvals

All of them pass → funded_verified_at is set. Only verified-funded films count toward L3/L4, maturity and spotlights. Faking this means paying real money and running a full production and review cycle, which costs more than the visibility is worth.

21.5 Mail volume, paid packs, data retention

  • Free allowance: 100 mails per creator per ISO week (Monday 00:00 UTC). Every recipient in a manual send uses one. Automatic and follow mails are platform-sent and do not use the creator's allowance.
  • Paid packs: when the free allowance runs out, the composer shows "Buy 500 more". tenant-service calls billing's addon-purchase with an idempotency key. Billing owns the price and debits the wallet as ADDON. On success, lead_mail_pack rows hold the credits, consumed oldest first after the free allowance. That revenue pays for extra sending capacity (a larger relay plan or a second mailbox) when volume needs it.
  • Retention: every rule (suppression, cooldowns, one-per-day) works on recipient_hash = sha256(normalised email + secret pepper). Raw email, name and company are needed only to render and send. A nightly job nulls them 30 days after delivery and sets pii_purged_at. Counts and attribution stay.
  • Cold-email law: low volume, at most one mail a day per brand, a 14-day same-creator cooldown, one-click unsubscribe, honest sender and subject, and a postal address. That is the respectful pattern the rules ask for.
